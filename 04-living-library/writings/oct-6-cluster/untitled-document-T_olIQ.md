# **​Proposal & Administrative Memorandum: Tribal Sovereignty, Jurisdictional Reciprocity, and Systemic Consistency**

​**Date:** October 5, 2026

**To:** The Fort Belknap Tribal Council & Community Stakeholders

**From:** Crystal Lee Pope & Community Supporters

## **​Executive Summary**

​This document synthesizes core recommendations regarding the restructuring of tribal jurisdictional boundaries, federal funding allocation mechanics, public assistance reciprocity, and standards for administrative record integrity. By establishing distinct sovereign governance while standardizing factual record-keeping and service reciprocity, both individual tribal nations and the broader community achieve true self-determination, administrative clarity, and equal protection under the law.

## **​Key Discussion Points & Structural Recommendations**

### **​1. Distinct Sovereignty & Equal Ruling Authority**

* ​**Independent Tribal Governance:** Formally establishing separate federal agency designations and independent jurisdictional boundaries for individual nations (such as the Assiniboine/Nakoda and Gros Ventre/Aaniiih) restores authentic self-determination.  
* ​**Equal Status:** Both nations sit as equal sovereign rulers over their respective heritages, moving away from forced joint governance toward voluntary, diplomatic inter-tribal agreements.

### **​2. Federal Funding Mechanics & Base Allocation Growth**

* ​**Direct Base Funding:** Federal funding formulas often include guaranteed "base allocations" per recognized tribal entity. Establishing independent tribal recognition allows each nation to receive its own full base allotment rather than dividing a single shared grant internally.  
* ​**Formula & Headcount Clarity:** Program funding tied to enrolled population or service area headcount is allocated directly to each nation's specific roster and land base, eliminating internal friction over resource distribution while doubling direct grant application opportunities.

### **​3. Service Reciprocity & Universal Access to Public Programs**

* ​**Seamless Public Assistance:** Federal and state safety-net programs—including SNAP, Medicaid, Indian Health Service (IHS), and commodities—must operate seamlessly across jurisdictional borders based on human need and residency, eliminating administrative delays or barriers.  
* ​**Credential & Jurisdiction Recognition:** Non-tribal state and federal agencies must be formally updated on tribal jurisdictional decisions to ensure tribal identification credentials and local authority are fully recognized and reciprocated across all borders.

### **​4. Linguistic Consistency & Fair Notice**

* ​**Universal Standard of Communication:** Communication relies on a shared, predictable code. Utilizing clear, standardized definitions (such as plain-language dictionary standards or explicit, transparent statutory definitions) ensures "fair notice."  
* ​**Eliminating Arbitrary Interpretation:** Uniform terminology prevents administrative clerks, agencies, or courts from applying rules subjectively, ensuring equal treatment across state, federal, and tribal administrative frameworks.

### **​5. Factual Integrity in Public Records & Lineal Descent**

* ​**Truth-in-Labeling Before Sealing:** Official public records, vital statistics, and agency documentation should require factual verification (such as genetic parentage/DNA testing) *prior* to being sealed as a complete record. Labeling an unverified claim creates false legal impressions that improperly shift evidentiary burdens onto individuals later.  
* ​**Lineal Descent Framework:** Transitioning tribal citizenship criteria to verified lineal descent resolves historical paper-record errors and arithmetic dilution associated with strict blood quantum models, anchoring sovereign membership in honest, objective lineage.

## **​Conclusion**

​Implementing distinct jurisdictional boundaries honors the fundamental right of tribal self-determination while building a cooperative, transparent foundation for the entire community. We respectfully request that the Tribal Council review these recommendations and include them on the official agenda for formal discussion.

Crystal Lee Pope
