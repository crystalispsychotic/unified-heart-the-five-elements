### **Plot Twist 1: The Victim Is the Perpetrator**

In a classic neo-noir setup, the detective is hired by a beautiful woman to investigate the murder of her wealthy husband. The husband was a seemingly ruthless businessman with plenty of enemies. The detective, a cynical ex-cop, gets pulled into a web of deceit, finding evidence that points to various suspects—rivals, jilted lovers, and disgruntled employees.  
The twist is that the "murder victim" is not actually dead. The husband and wife faked his death to collect a massive insurance payout and disappear with the money. The husband, the supposed victim, has been orchestrating the entire investigation from the shadows, feeding the detective false leads to make the case look genuine. The wife hired the detective not to find her husband's killer, but to make sure the "investigation" was convincing enough to fool the insurance company and the police.  
The moment of revelation could come when the detective discovers a hidden communication channel between the wife and an anonymous figure, leading him to a secluded location where the "victim" is alive and well, preparing to escape. The detective now has a choice: expose their scheme, risking his own life and the chance of a big reward, or take a cut of the money and let them go.

### **Plot Twist 2: The "Hero" Is the Real Villain**

A down-on-his-luck private eye, haunted by a past case that went wrong, is hired to find a missing child. The case seems straightforward at first: a wealthy family's child has disappeared, and the police have hit a dead end. The detective, driven by guilt and a need for redemption, takes the case personally.  
As he investigates, he uncovers a conspiracy involving local politicians and a powerful corporation. He finds evidence that they are behind the kidnapping, planning to use the child as leverage in a shady real estate deal. The detective races against time, fighting off hired thugs and outsmarting corrupt cops to save the child. The audience is rooting for him every step of the way, believing he is the last good man left in a corrupt city.  
The twist is that the detective is not a hero at all. He is a disgraced former cop who was fired for his role in a similar kidnapping years ago. The current case is not a chance at redemption but a calculated act of revenge. He staged the kidnapping himself, using his inside knowledge to create a believable mystery. The "conspiracy" is a fabrication, and he's been the puppet master all along, using the missing child to get back at the people who wronged him in the past.  
The final scene reveals the detective holding the child captive, not to save them, but to make a final, damning statement to the city that ruined his life. The truth is that the "missing child" is a decoy, and the real child is being held somewhere else. The detective is not a protector, but a master manipulator, and the line between good and evil has been completely blurred.
