**1\. Alice in Wonderland Syndrome (AIWS):**

> * **What it is:** A rare neurological condition that affects perception, causing distortions in the size, shape, or distance of objects, or one's own body parts. You might see things as larger (macropsia) or smaller (micropsia), closer (pelopsia) or further away (telopsia). Time perception can also be distorted.

> * **Key characteristic:** People experiencing AIWS are typically aware that their perceptions are distorted and not real.

**2\. Depersonalization-Derealization Disorder (DPDR):**

> * **What it is:** A dissociative disorder characterized by persistent or recurrent feelings of detachment from one's own body, thoughts, feelings, or actions (depersonalization) and/or a sense of unreality or detachment from one's surroundings (derealization).  
  * **Depersonalization:** Feeling like an outside observer of your own thoughts, feelings, body, or actions. You might feel like a robot, numb, or that your body parts are twisted or not the right shape/size.  
  * **Derealization:** Feeling that people and your surroundings aren't real, like you're living in a dream or a movie. The world might appear blurry, colorless, or two-dimensional.  
> * **Triggers:** Often triggered by severe stress or trauma, but can also occur without a clear cause.

**Potential Overlap between AIWS and DPDR:**

> * AIWS can sometimes include symptoms of depersonalization and derealization. Both involve perceptual distortions, though AIWS is more focused on visual and body-size distortions, while DPDR is about a sense of unreality or detachment.
